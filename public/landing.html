Productivity Tips That Actually Work

At Taskflow, we've studied the most effective productivity techniques and built them directly into the app. Here's what the research says about staying focused and getting more done every day.

The Pomodoro Technique: Work in Focused Sprints

Developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, the Pomodoro Technique involves working in 25-minute focused sessions separated by 5-minute breaks. After four sessions, you take a longer 30-minute break. Research from the University of Illinois found that brief mental breaks significantly improve focus over long tasks. Taskflow's built-in Focus Timer is designed around this principle.

Why Habit Stacking Beats Willpower Every Time

Willpower is a limited resource that depletes throughout the day. The science-backed alternative is habit stacking — attaching new behaviours to existing ones. James Clear's research shows it takes an average of 66 days to form a habit. Taskflow's Habit Tracker helps you build consistency with streaks, reminders, and visual progress that keeps you accountable even when motivation is low.

The Eisenhower Matrix: Prioritise What Actually Matters

President Dwight Eisenhower famously said what is important is seldom urgent, and what is urgent is seldom important. The matrix divides tasks into four quadrants: Urgent and Important (do now), Not Urgent but Important (schedule), Urgent but Not Important (delegate), and Neither (eliminate). Taskflow's High, Medium, Low priority system maps directly onto this framework.

How Mood Tracking Improves Productivity

A 2019 study published in PLOS ONE found that tracking your mood for just a few weeks leads to measurable improvements in emotional self-awareness and decision-making. When you notice patterns — perhaps you are always low-energy on Monday afternoons — you can structure your most important tasks around your natural energy cycles. Taskflow's Mood Journal reveals these patterns automatically.
